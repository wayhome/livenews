Orphan Trains

作者: brudgers | 评分: 3 | 评论数: 1 | 发布时间: 2025-07-06 20:55
文章摘要

The Orphan Train Movement (1854-1929) was a U.S. welfare program that relocated about 200,000 children from crowded Eastern cities to rural areas in the Midwest to address labor shortages. Though portrayed as orphans or homeless, many children were from poor immigrant families. Critics argue the program lacked proper screening and follow-ups, with some children exploited as farm labor. Key organizations, like the Children's Aid Society founded by Charles Loring Brace, facilitated these placements, aiming to provide children with better lives in rural homes. The term "orphan train" became widely recognized only after the program ended, with the movement serving as an early form of foster care.
