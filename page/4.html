文章摘要

The Trump administration tasked a Department of Government Efficiency engineer with no healthcare or government experience to develop an AI tool to identify non-essential Veterans Affairs (VA) contracts for cancellation. The AI tool, built quickly using outdated and inexpensive models, made significant errors, such as inflating contract values. It flagged over 2,000 contracts as "munchable," including crucial ones for cancer research and healthcare improvements. Experts criticized the use of AI for such complex decisions, calling it "deeply problematic" and unreliable. Despite acknowledging flaws, the engineer, Sahil Lavingia, defended the rushed process due to a tight 30-day review period mandated by a Trump executive order. The extent of contract cancellations remains unclear, but nearly 600 have been killed, raising concerns about the impact on veteran care.
