文章摘要

Matt Zajack, a data analytics consultant from Portland, has developed an affordable, open-source **Traffic Monitor** device aimed at revolutionizing urban planning and advocacy for healthier cities. Mounted on his garage, the device uses a camera, Raspberry Pi computer, and AI to track and display real-time data on bikes, pedestrians, and cars passing by. It can also measure air quality, temperature, and vehicle speed using additional sensors like Doppler radar. Zajack's goal is to provide communities and advocacy groups with accessible, low-cost traffic data to support initiatives such as traffic calming and to challenge sparse or outdated municipal data. The device costs about $400 to build and can be easily moved to different locations, offering a comprehensive view of street activity, promoting human-friendly urban environments.
