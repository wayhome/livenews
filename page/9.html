文章摘要

ESA's Gaia satellite, launched in 2013 to map the Milky Way in 3D, has been retired after mapping over two billion stars and making significant discoveries, such as the galaxy's wobble and the closest black hole to Earth. Designed to be highly resilient with multiple backup systems, Gaia proved challenging to shut down. Engineers systematically disabled its redundant systems and corrupted software to ensure it wouldn't reactivate. The spacecraft is now in a safe solar orbit, and its data, a treasure trove for astronomy, will continue to influence future research. A successor, GaiaNIR, is planned to continue similar mapping in different wavelengths.
