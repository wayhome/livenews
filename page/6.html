The nine-armed octopus and the oddities of the cephalopod nervous system

作者: mdp2021 | 评分: 6 | 评论数: 2 | 发布时间: 2025-06-08 04:12
文章摘要

Octopuses fascinate both the public and scientists due to their intelligence, quick-change camouflage, and abilities such as recognizing faces, solving puzzles, and learning from others. Their complex nervous system, with about 500 million neurons, is the most advanced among invertebrates. Notably, 350 million neurons are in their arms, allowing each arm to function semi-independently with its own "mini-brain." This decentralized system enables fast responses and greater adaptability, such as continuing to operate if an arm is injured. Each sucker on their arms also contains a nerve center, adding to their neural complexity. Despite this independence, the central brain coordinates overall movement and handles tasks like navigation and learning. Studying octopus neuroscience is challenging due to their unique body structure, but it offers insights into their evolution and resilience in the ocean ecosystem.
